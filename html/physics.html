THE FIELD

What is this field?

⚛️

Physics + Mathematics + Computing

Computational physics uses mathematics, algorithms and computers to model physical systems that may be difficult, expensive or impossible to study directly in the real world.

Instead of only solving equations on paper, you can build simulations that show how particles, fluids, spacecraft, forces, waves and complex systems behave over time.

02

WHY IT MATTERS

Why does this field matter?

🔬

Understand Complex Systems

Computers can model systems containing thousands or millions of interacting variables.

🚀

Simulations allow engineers and scientists to test ideas before spending resources on physical experiments.

🧠

Equations become moving particles, flowing fluids, trajectories, waves and visual models.

FEATURED TOOLS

Tools worth exploring

⚙️
NUMERICAL COMPUTING

GNU Octave

A free and open-source mathematical programming environment designed for numerical calculations.

What exactly does it do?

Octave lets you work with vectors, matrices, equations, graphs and numerical algorithms. It is especially useful when you want to translate mathematical ideas into code.

How does it work?

You provide mathematical expressions or algorithms. Octave calculates the results and can visualize the data using graphs and plots.

t = 0:0.01:10;
y = sin(t);
plot(t, y);
🌊
FLUID DYNAMICS

Lattice

A computational environment for exploring fluid behaviour and numerical simulation concepts.

What can you study?

You can explore how fluids move, interact and change under different conditions using computational models.

🚀
AEROSPACE

LaunchSim

A simulation-oriented environment for understanding rocket launches, trajectories and aerospace concepts.

What can you explore?

Experiment with concepts such as thrust, gravity, velocity, altitude and trajectory.

PROS & CONS

Advantages & limitations

✓ Advantages

  • Can explore systems without physical experiments
  • Can repeat experiments quickly
  • Works with very complex mathematical models
  • Useful across science and engineering
  • Can visualize invisible physical processes
  • Open-source tools can be free to use

⚠ Limitations

  • A simulation is an approximation of reality
  • Incorrect assumptions produce incorrect results
  • Advanced simulations can require powerful hardware
  • Mathematics can become highly advanced
  • Large simulations can take significant computing time

DID YOU KNOW?

Things people often don't realize

01

A computer simulation does not automatically represent reality perfectly. The assumptions inside the model matter.

02

Many physical equations cannot be solved exactly, so numerical methods are used to approximate their solutions.

03

Small changes in some systems can produce dramatically different outcomes — a key idea behind chaos theory.

04

Simulation connects subjects that students often learn separately: physics, mathematics and programming.
